Synth Workspace Bridge — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 4 November 2026

The Synth Workspace Bridge extension (“the extension”) connects your browser to a Synth Workspace deployment that you choose. This policy covers the extension only. The Workspace deployment you pair with has its own data handling, set by whoever operates it.

Contact: syntithenai@gmail.com

Summary

  • The extension has no servers of its own. It does not send data to the developer.
  • There is no analytics, telemetry, advertising, or crash reporting.
  • Two kinds of data move through the extension, and both go only to a destination you chose.

1. Pairing

The extension stores one thing: a random pairing token in chrome.storage.local. The Workspace stores the same token encrypted on your device and presents it with every request. The token identifies the connection, not you. There is no account, email address, or identity in the extension.

Revoking the token on the Options page, or uninstalling the extension, deletes it.

2. Browser control

When you, or an assistant acting on your request, ask the Workspace to act in a tab, the extension may read:

  • the tab's URL and title;
  • the page's visible text, links, and form-field labels;
  • a PNG screenshot of the visible tab.

It may also click an element, type into a non-password field, press Enter, navigate to a URL, open, close, or activate a tab.

That data goes only to the paired Workspace origin — https://suite.synthfit.online or a localhost development origin. The extension does not store page content, and does not send it to the developer or to any third party.

Limits built into the extension:

  • password fields are never read or filled;
  • the debugger is attached for one action and detached immediately afterwards;
  • browser-internal pages (chrome://), the Chrome Web Store, and other extensions cannot be controlled;
  • a click can be pinned to the text and link you approved, so a page that changed underneath is reported rather than clicked;
  • page text is treated as untrusted input by the Workspace.

3. Model requests (bring your own key)

If you use the extension to route model calls, it handles:

  • the request body and model name produced by your Workspace;
  • the provider API key you configured in the Workspace.

They go only to the model-provider endpoint you selected. The key exists in memory for the duration of one request; the extension never writes it to storage and never logs it. The extension does not otherwise receive your chat history — it relays exactly the request the Workspace sends.

Providers process those requests under their own privacy policies. The extension's provider allowlist is published in its manifest.json and shown on the Options page.

4. What the extension does not do

  • It does not sell or share data for advertising or credit.
  • It does not use analytics, telemetry, or crash reporting.
  • It does not read tabs in the background. Nothing happens until an action is requested.
  • It does not download or execute remote code. Every expression it evaluates is a constant in its own bundle; the paired page cannot supply a script.
  • It does not read cookies, saved passwords, or browsing history.

5. Limited Use

The extension's use of data complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data is used only to provide the extension's single purpose: connecting your browser to your own Workspace so the Workspace can act through your signed-in tabs and route model requests through your connection.

6. Retention

The extension retains only the pairing token, until you revoke it or uninstall the extension. It retains no page content, model requests, or provider keys.

7. Your controls

  • Revoke pairing: Options page → Revoke. Every page is refused immediately.
  • Revoke provider access: chrome://extensions → Details → Site access, or uninstall the extension.
  • Remove everything: uninstalling the extension deletes its storage.

8. Children

The extension is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.

Material changes will be noted in the release notes and reflected in the date above.
